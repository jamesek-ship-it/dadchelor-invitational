The Tournament

Welcome to The Dadchelor

Each year, a band of brothers — husbands, fathers, and former high school athletes — converge upon a storied Michigan golf course for the most prestigious tournament no one outside their group has ever heard of.

What started as a trip to Treetops in 2019 to celebrate Daniel Harras (The Original Dadchelor, D-Raw, Dan-o, Liar Dan) on the eve of fatherhood gave birth to something in its own right — an institution, an annual summer fixture among friends, The Dadchelor Invitational: complete with the Dadchelor Chalice (beginning in 2026, the New Era), team bragging rights, official tournament records (also beginning in 2026), player announcements, and the kind of competitive fire that only comes from years of shared friendship, trash talk, and the occasional three-putt.

About The Dadchelor

History · Traditions · The Spirit of the Game

Our Story

A Brotherhood Forged in Fairways

The Dadchelor Invitational was born from a simple idea: that a group of lifelong friends — now husbands, fathers, and reluctant adults — deserved one weekend a year to reclaim their inner competitor, swing some clubs, and remind one another exactly who the alpha golfer of the group really is.

What started as a casual Michigan golf trip has grown into a full-fledged institution. Each year, before a single ball is struck, every competitor is formally announced on the first tee — their accomplishments, accolades, and deeply questionable life decisions read aloud for the entertainment of the assembled field.

The champion earns the Dadchelor Chalice, the right to select the following year's menu, and bragging rights that must be surrendered at the next edition.

The tournament has been held at some of Michigan's finest golf destinations, with the field growing in size and competitive intensity with each passing year.

And then came 2026.

The eighth edition marks the dawn of a new chapter — The Modern Era of The Dadchelor Invitational. For the first time, players do not merely compete for Lil and Big Peen honors, for Look Good and Feel Good accolades, or for the right to have their biography read aloud on the first tee. They now contend for something greater: the title of Champion Golfer of the Year.

The Champion Golfer of the Year is a man who will be immortalized for all time — a man who has earned the right, proudly and without apology, to ask the question that echoes across every fairway, every green, and every 19th hole deliberation: "Who's your Daddy?!"

The Modern Era is ushered in partly through the power of artificial intelligence — bringing full website integration, live scoring through Bogey Golf, and a rich digital tournament experience that would have been unthinkable in the early editions. The Dadchelor has grown up. The game has changed. The stakes have never been higher.

Champion Golfer of the Year

For the first time in Dadchelor history, players compete not just for team glory, Dadchelor Chalice rights, or first-tee bragging rights — but for an individual title that stands above all others. The Champion Golfer of the Year earns the right to ask one question, loudly and without apology:

The defending champion earns the privilege to choose the food and beverage menu at the following year's tournament, and pay for said menu at his own expense.

The Ryder Cup Format

Team Look Good vs Team Feel Good. Four rounds across two days. Fourball, Scramble, and Individual Match Play. The team with the most points lifts the Dadchelor Cup. Every stroke, every putt, every conceded hole counts toward both the team battle and the individual race.

Tournament Lore

The Honors

Lil Peen & Big Peen

Lil Peen and Big Peen were the original team names for the first two Dadchelors — irreverent, perfectly on brand, and exactly what you'd expect. They were eventually rebranded to Look Good and Feel Good to be more palatable to potential trip sponsors. We never got the sponsors. We kept the names anyway.

The Announcements

Before a single ball is struck, the field assembles on the first tee. One by one, each competitor is formally announced — their accomplishments, their accolades, their life milestones, and their most deeply questionable decisions read aloud for the entertainment of everyone present. The crowd does not always believe everything they hear. The competitor has no right of reply.

    Rules & Scoring

    By Which The Game Is Played

    Official

    Tournament Rules

    The Captaincy
    1
    Captains must be present for all rounds of the tournament.
    2
    The highest point scorer on both teams becomes team Captain for the following year.
    3
    Team captains must have their picture taken before the first tee as a matter of sportsmanship before trash talk commences.
    4
    Team Captains shall be the sole representative for each team concerning tournament matters. Captains may designate an Alternate Captain and delegate the powers of the Captain as appropriate. Should anything happen to the Captain (injuries, hangovers, etc.) the Alternate Captain shall assume all powers endowed to the Captain.
    Rule Submission & Ratification
    5
    Rules may be submitted by any tournament participant of three (3) or more years.
    6
    Rules shall become ratified subject to a 2/3 vote.
    7
    The Commissioner shall abstain from all votes, except when his participation is needed to break a 2/3 tie-breaking vote.
    8
    Rules may be vetoed by the Commissioner. A unanimous vote (of the non-Commissioner votes) overrides the veto and the rule becomes law.
    9
    Rules within a match may be altered upon unanimous vote of the group (breakfast ball policies, the wearing of shoes, etc).
    10
    Rule submissions are accepted throughout the calendar year, but all rule deliberation and voting shall take place during the tournament weekend, in the presence of all eligible participants, presided over by the Commissioner.

    How Points Work

    The Scoring

    19 total points across the weekend — 15 from match play, 4 from side contests.

    Match Play · 15 pts

    Every match is worth 1 team point, awarded to the winning team. A halved match splits the point — ½ to each team.

    Round Format Matches Pts
    R1 — Huron Breeze, Fri AMFourball (2v2 & 1v1)33
    R2 — Red Hawk, Fri PMBattle of the Dans + two 2v2 matches33
    R3 — The Dream, Sat AMFourball (2v2)33
    R4 — The Nightmare, Sat PMIndividual match play, seeded by R3 standings66

    Results are recorded in standard match play notation — "4 & 3," "2 up," "Halved." A match is decided once the leader's margin exceeds the holes remaining, or after all 18 are played.

    Side Contests · 4 pts

    Four contests — Closest to the Pin and Long Drive, one of each on Friday's two courses. Each is worth 1 team point to the winner's team, plus individual recognition for that player.

    Champion Golfer of the Year (Individual)
    • In a 2-man team match (fourball, 2v2 scramble), a win credits each partner the full point individually — a 2v2 win is worth 1 pt to the team and 1 pt to each of the two players.
    • In a singles match (Battle of the Dans, Round 4), the point belongs solely to the individual winner.
    • Side contest wins add a bonus to that player's individual total, shown separately in the standings.
    • Halved matches split identically at the individual level — ½ point to each player involved.

    A player's CGOY total = points earned from their own match record + any CTP/LD bonus. Tracked independently of team scoring — one guy can lead the individual race while playing for the team that's behind.

    Round 4 Seeding

    Standings after Round 3 rank each team's six players. Round 4 pairs #1 vs #1, #2 vs #2, straight down to #6 vs #6 — always Look Good vs Feel Good. Two players who only play Saturday carry a +1 seeding adjustment that affects bracket placement only — it never touches their actual points or the leaderboard.
